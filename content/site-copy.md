**Intro**
A lot of the people I work with aren't in Las Vegas when they start looking. So I go walk it and
send you the video. You see the condition of the floors, what the kitchen actually is, whether the
back yard has anything in it. Photos on a listing are shot to sell it. The video is just the house.

**Body: what I look at**

baseboards are two-inch or five-inch. Whether the cabinets are original and painted, or actually

still pay for one of those — but it tells you what kind of house you're standing in before you
spend the money.

give access, then nobody's walking it and I'll tell you that up front instead of guessing about
what's inside.

## SELL  (assessment register)

**H1**
Selling a house that needs work

**H2**
I'll price it twice: as it sits today, and after a repair list I can actually put numbers to.

**Intro**
Most people call me because the house isn't in shape to photograph. It's a rental that just
turned over, or a property from an estate, or something that's been closed up. That's fine. I'd
rather look at it dirty than have you spend money on the wrong things first.

**Body: the two numbers**
Here's what I do. I walk it, and I write down what's original and what's been replaced. Then I
get my guys to price the repairs that matter — paint, flooring, the cabinets if they're gone, the
baseboards if they've been painted twenty times. You end up with two numbers: what it's worth
today, and what it's probably worth after the work, minus the work. Sometimes the second number
is barely bigger than the first, and then I tell you to sell it as-is and keep your money.

**Body: the decision is yours**
I do consider a full remodel on most of these, and a lot of the time I don't recommend it. If the
floor plan is the problem, paint doesn't fix it. You could knock a wall out, but that's a real
budget and it isn't always worth it. I'll give you the numbers and you decide how far you want to go.

**Honest limitation**
The repair numbers are estimates from the trades I use, on the day they looked at it. Material
prices move. If the scope changes once a wall is open, the number changes with it.

## INVEST  (assessment register)

**H1**
Buying property to hold or flip

**H2**
Rentals, auction buys, and 1031 exchanges. I'll tell you what the condition is going to cost you.

**Intro**
I buy and renovate property myself, so when I look at a deal I'm looking at it the way I'd look
at one of mine. What's the roof. What's the plumbing. What did the last owner do himself that's
going to have to come out.

### What I actually do on these

**Rentals that won't stay filled**
A unit that keeps going vacant usually has a reason, and it's usually visible. Blinds that are
thrashed. A shower with no door on it. Burnt-out bulbs and a smell nobody dealt with. I'll walk
it and list what has to be fixed before it will rent, and roughly what that costs.

**Auctions**
I'll pull what's available on the property and tell you what I can and can't confirm. On most
auction properties nobody gets inside first, so you're buying condition you haven't seen. I'm not
going to pretend otherwise.

**1031 exchanges**
I handle the real estate side — finding the replacement property and getting it closed inside the
timeline. The exchange itself runs through a qualified intermediary, and the tax treatment is
between you and your CPA. I don't give tax advice and I'll say so before you ask.

**Honest limitation**
I don't do projections. I'm not going to tell you what a property will appreciate or what it'll
rent for in three years, because I don't know, and neither does anybody who tells you they do.

## RENOVATIONS  (assessment register for scope; presentation register for the finished-work module)

> R3: this route replaces `/property-help`. The label "Renovations" is exact and mandatory.

**H1**
Renovations

**H2**
Vacant, tired, or half-finished properties, mostly for owners who aren't in town.

**Intro**
This started because people kept calling me about houses they own and can't get to. Somebody
inherited a place in Las Vegas and lives in another state. A rental came back in bad shape. A
contractor took a deposit and stopped showing up. I go look at it, film it, and tell you what
it needs.

### What condition usually looks like

**Body**
I've walked into houses with cabinets Mickey Moused together, floors that are totally unlevel,
baseboards painted so many times they're rounded over. Some of it is salvageable and some of it
isn't. A porcelain tub can usually be reglazed instead of replaced. Lower cabinets that are
chewed up aren't worth saving. I'll tell you which is which before you spend anything.

### How the work gets done

**Body**
I don't hold a contractor's license and I don't swing a hammer. I have trades I use — a tile
installer, a guy who does my door casings, painters — and I arrange the work and check it. They
do a really nice job and I'd rather credit them than pretend it's me. You'll be paying them, and
you'll know what each piece costs.

> ⚠ **SCOPE GATE (R7 / brief §21.5, Appendix E item 9).** This page is written in coordination
> language throughout. If Jason confirms in writing that he holds a Nevada contractor's license,
> or that all work is performed by licensed third parties under a documented arrangement, this
> module can be rewritten. Until then, "we install" wording must not be published.

### Decisions I'll usually push back on

**Body**
I do consider a full gut on most of these, and I usually don't recommend it. Five-inch baseboards
look a lot better than two-inch, and it's a bit more expensive material, so it depends what you're
spending overall. If the ground cover is bare dirt I'd generally leave it and give a credit
instead of picking gravel or turf, because that's a personal choice and I don't want to pick for
the next owner.

**Honest limitation**
I can't supervise a job every day. I check the work at the stages that matter and I'll send you
photos, but if you need somebody on site full time, that's a project manager and it isn't me.

## ABOUT  (presentation register, first person throughout)

**H1**
About me

**H2**
I'm a salesperson with Blue Diamond Realty, and I buy and fix property in Las Vegas myself.

**Intro**
The short version: I list and sell houses, I renovate them, and I work on the ones other agents
don't want to deal with. Probates. Auctions. Quiet title. Rentals that keep going empty. A lot of
that work is slow and unglamorous and I don't mind it.

**Body: how I work**
I film everything. If I'm going to tell you a house is in good shape, I'd rather show you than
say it. When I walk a property I mark what's new and what's existing as I go, because that's the
part people get wrong — a kitchen can look brand new and be the same cabinets painted white.
That's not a bad thing, but you should know which one you're looking at.

**Body: what I don't do**
I'm not a home inspector. I'm not a contractor, an attorney, or a CPA. On a probate, the attorney
runs the probate. On a 1031, your CPA handles the tax side. On quiet title, that's a lawsuit and
a lawyer files it. I do the real estate part and I'll tell you plainly when you've reached the
edge of it.

**Honest limitation**
I'm one person with a phone and a truck. If you need same-day access to six properties across the
valley, I'm probably not your guy that week.
